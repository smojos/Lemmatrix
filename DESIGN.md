## 1. What we're building

- **The goal** (Josh, 21 October; the slogan for the documentation and descriptions): ***Human readable, machine processable, ethically collected and shared linguistic data.*** The database holds what describes the data or is needed to read it; working state goes elsewhere (the workspace file, IDEAS.md idea 36), and so do the researcher's own ideas (the notebook, below).
- **The data, and the researcher's own work** (Josh, 22 October): Lemmatrix keeps two kinds of thing apart, and the difference runs through the whole design.
  - **The data** is what was collected and what describes it: lemmas and their tokens with their recordings, utterances, texts with their TextGrids, the fields, tags and lists that describe them (the tone classes, the word classes, the reference categories), metadata, and the consent checkboxes that say how it may be shared. It lives in the database file and its soundfiles. It is what gets archived and shared: *ethically collected and shared*.
  - **The researcher's own work** is what they make of the data: the notebook's notes, questions and To Dos, Cogitate's hypotheses with their evidence, the workspace (what they are working on), and later Analyze's results and Compose's papers. These are the researcher's academic ideas, their private property, not data for public sharing: they go into every Full backup, so nothing is lost, but never into an archive unless the researcher chooses to put them there. Others can look at the archived data and come to their own conclusions; they may not piggyback on unpublished thoughts.
  - So everything new is asked which side it is on. Data goes in the database. The researcher's own work goes in files beside it (the notebook, the workspace; later `Papers/` and the analyses), which the archives leave out (section 3.15). Both sides are brought together on screen, since that is the point of Lemmatrix (the notebook's links, Cogitate's evidence, “In hypotheses” in the dialogues), but they are stored and shared apart.
- **Lemmatrix** is a program for phonological and grammatical analysis and linguistic documentation (any language), built from scratch and inspired by tools such as FLEx, Praat, ELAN, Dekereke and Excel. **No code from any of them is reused.**
- **Licence** (Josh, 23 October, from version 0.2.0): free and open source under the GNU Affero General Public License, version 3 or later (**AGPL-3.0-or-later**), © 2026 Joshua A. G. Smolders; versions 0.1.0–0.1.2 were GPL-3.0-or-later. Josh's four aims: to be known for Lemmatrix, to protect its name, to keep it free and usable at no charge, and to do it without much expense. So:
  - **AGPL**, not GPL, so that a web or cloud version built from Lemmatrix must also share its code.
  - **Additional terms** (`NOTICE.md`, the licence's section 7): every copy and modified version keeps *Based on Lemmatrix by Joshua A. G. Smolders (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23512411)* in its About screen and README (7(b)); a modified version says it has been changed (7(c)); and it takes a name of its own (7(e)). Every licensed file's header points to `NOTICE.md`.
  - **The artwork is all rights reserved** (`ARTWORK.md`): the icons, the splash screen and the ɛλicit drawings, including the icons drawn in code (`ui/icons.py`, `ui/whiteboard_art.py`, whose SPDX line is `LicenseRef-Lemmatrix-Artwork`). Unmodified copies may be shared with it; a distributed modified version must make its own icons for everything. **New artwork goes in those two modules (or `resources/` or `docs/elicit/`), never into ordinary AGPL code.** ARTWORK.md and SAMPLES.md reserve **folders and modules**, so anything new put there is covered at once; `tests/test_reserved_places.py` (26 October) fails if an image, recording, PDF or TextGrid is anywhere else (committed or not), or a reserved module loses its header or isn't named in its terms. Never widen the test's list to let a file through: move the file.
  - **The sample databases are all rights reserved too** (`SAMPLES.md`; Josh, 25 October: *they can take the program and its utility, but they cannot take credit for my artistic spirit*): the sample (`Sample.xml`, its workspace and `Soundfiles/`) and the ɛλicit test database (`samples/Elicit test/`), and the scripts that build them, `samples/make_sample.py` and `samples/make_elicit_test.py` (SPDX `LicenseRef-Lemmatrix-Samples`), since they hold the invented language and its texts; so a fork can't rebuild them under the AGPL. Unmodified copies may be shared with them; a distributed modified version must make sample data of its own.   - **Free, always:** the README says so, and the official homes are the GitHub repository and Zenodo.
  - See also `AUTHORS`, `CITATION.cff` and `THIRD-PARTY.md`. **`.zenodo.json`** (23 October, before the 0.2.0 release) gives Zenodo the record's title, creator, version, licence and a note on what isn't under the AGPL (the artwork, the EACW's CC BY 4.0, the CAWL as numbers only); Zenodo reads it in place of CITATION.cff, so the two are kept in step (`tests/test_release.py`). - **The name** (Josh, 24 October; the README's *The name*): *Lemmatrix* is a portmanteau of **lemma** and **matrix**: the system is built on lemmas as the head words, and on matrices that let you look at them in meaningful paradigms and comparisons. The second spelling, **λemmatriχ** (a lambda at the beginning, a chi at the end; the splash screen's title), shows it visually: replacing the L and the X with these letters gives the idea of the Y and the X axes, since a lowercase lambda looks like an upside-down Y, and the chi is the X. And the matrices can be flipped, swapping the X and Y axes, conveniently (section 5.4). That is the Easter egg (Josh, 26 October): the Y is already upside down, as if its table had just been flipped.
- **Terminology:**
  - The basic unit is a **database**, not a "lexicon".
  - Each entry is organised under a **lemma** (not "headword").
  - The right-hand view of tokens is the **matrix view**.
  - **The phonetics scratchpad** (Josh, 24 October): the Pitch and Segments scratchpads together, α and β (and γ) for asking "is it A or B?" (section 5.1); called the *comparison area* until then.
  - **The parts of a window** (Josh, 24 October), names to use from now on: the table or matrix itself is the **working window** (the lemma list, All data, a matrix, the Utterances grid, a text's lines); the area under it is its **working panel**; the strip of icons along the top of a working panel is its **options bar**; the strip of 48 px icons and view tabs above a working window is the **app bar**. Older text here says *options bar* or *options panel* for the working panel; read it so.
  - **soundfile** and **soundfiles**, always one word (like *filename*), in the program, its messages, comments and documentation (Josh, 20 October). `tests/test_terms.py` fails if it is ever written as two words.
  - **A tick is a bug** (*I found a tick!*), and only that (Josh, 24 October). A checkbox is **selected** or **deselected** (Select all shown, Deselect all), never ticked; the rows highlighted in a table are **highlighted** where both come up together (Bulk edit's *Deselect highlighted rows*). A ✓ drawn in an icon is a **checkmark**. `tests/test_terms.py` fails if the program's text says tick for anything but the bug.
- **Why a new program?** Other tools which I previously used had either limited or unideal functionality, with constant import and export stages creating scattered files, databases and analyses. I found that FLEx was extremely slow compared to a modern Python-based app, too slow in navigation and filtering for efficient data collection (work is much more efficient in Lemmatrix), relied too much on hand editing, its XML output was not human readable, its auto-recorded soundfiles were not easily readable updatable or searchable, and its text analysis could not interact directly with soundfiles. Praat TextGrids are too sensitive when editing, extremely reliant on hand editing, and could not communicate between texts. Dekereke's data model is flat (one row per record), its pitch transcription relies on a font rather than hard underlying data, it cannot handle multiple paradigms in elicitation in a single database without great effort, and it could not interact with text data. All of these were disconnected from each other, and from the notes and questions arising from the various analyses. The core goal here is a two-level model, with lemmas and tokens beneath them, stored as readable XML without repeated data, completely searchable and smartly bulk editable, with all the analysis and export tools a linguist could hope for. In addition it is meant to organize texts and bring them to bear in lexical and grammatical analysis, all while recording the whole process and ensuring modern ethical standards are being employed.
  - The notes, questions and hypotheses are brought to the data but stored apart from it: they belong to the researcher, while the data is what is archived and shared (above). The consent checkboxes decide who may be named and whose voice may be heard when it is.
- **Preserve and protect the data** (Josh, 23 October): a fundamental principle. All systems are designed never to change the data unintentionally. Lemmatrix never touches your diacritics except when you bulk edit them. Views, filters and hiding (the pitch system, section 3.5; second languages; Stem type and From) change only what is shown, never what is stored.
- **Order is the computer's job** (Josh, 23 October): In other programs I had to rename soundfiles by hand, or open up the XML in Notepad++ to reorder examples by hand, so my OCD wasn't triggered knowing they were underlyingly out of order. Linguists should not have to waste time and energy bringing order to their data when computers can do it for them. So Lemmatrix names soundfiles from the data and renames them when the data changes (section 3.3; Tools → Update all soundfile names), keeps the lemmas of a wordlist database in the list's order as they are added, inserted or converted, and puts any stragglers back on request (Tools → Check reference order…, section 3.16).
- **Fast processing** (Josh, 25 October): Lemmatrix must stay quick with a fully collected comparative wordlist, all ~2,500 EACW items with an average of a dozen tokens or more each. It is measured, not assumed, on an invented database of that size (section 5.7, Speed at full size).
- **Catching mistakes before they need to be bulk edited** (Josh, 25 October): a mistake caught as it is typed costs nothing; one found in 60,000 tokens costs a Bulk edit. So Lemmatrix prefers to help at the moment of entry: the linguist-friendly autocorrect (IDEAS.md idea 21), the pitch-looking-text warning (section 5.6), the same-configuration box, the spelling underlines (5.10).
- **Descriptive adequacy first** (Josh, 22 October): the documentary capabilities of this program were inspired particularly by my supervisor, Bert Remijsen, before he was even my supervisor, when in 2024 he said to me in a Zoom call, *“We strive first for descriptive adequacy.”* So Lemmatrix records the data fully and faithfully (every token with its recording, its pitch and its context) before it helps with any analysis of it, and the roadmap follows the same order: Collect before Analyze, Analyze before Compose (section 6).
- **Platforms:** Windows first, then Mac and Linux, because colleagues use both.
- **Audience:** field linguists. Free to use, share and change under the licence.
- **A UI for every kind of linguist** (Josh, 23 October): One of the main goals of this program is to make the UI extremely versatile and user friendly, for those linguists who are a little more on the artistic end of the spectrum like myself.
- **Manageable data** (Josh, 24 October): Linguists want to feel like their data is manageable, especially at first. So Lemmatrix shows what is needed now and keeps the rest within reach: the lemma dialogue's More arrow and the gloss's + (section 3.2), Hide empty lemmas (5.2), the EACW's Base list before Expanded (3.16), the pitch system that can be hidden (3.5). A new dialogue or view starts with the few things most people fill in, and nothing is lost by folding the rest away.

## 2. Technology and setup

- **Language:** Python 3.13 with Qt for Python (PySide6 6.12). It was chosen because it runs on all three platforms, is the most readable option for a non-programmer, and can be fully built and tested in Claude's workspace, including screenshots of the windows.
- **Audio:** `sounddevice` / `soundcard` for recording and playback, including capturing computer audio on Windows.
- **English inflection:** `lemminflect` (MIT licence, offline) for predictive translation (section 5.9).
- **English spelling:** `pyspellchecker` (MIT licence, offline) together with `lemminflect` (section 5.10).
- **Acoustic analysis (later):** `parselmouth` (Praat in Python). It's GPL-licensed, which can be combined with Lemmatrix's AGPL (section 13 of both licences).
- **Fonts:** Charis SIL. Tone letters render as contours, with the staves hidden through the font's `cv92` feature. This was tested and works in Qt.
- **Running it on Windows:** `Run Lemmatrix.bat` creates the program's own Python 3.13 environment with `py -3.13` (a plain `python` may be an older version) and installs the packages on the first run.
- **Development:** the author designs and specifies Lemmatrix and tests every version on his own data; the code is written with an AI coding assistant (Claude, by Anthropic), which also writes and runs the automated tests. The code is kept on GitHub and pulled with GitHub Desktop.
- **Data safety:** readable XML, version history, and automated tests (especially for import and save).
- **Running the tests:** `Run tests.bat` (or `python -m pytest tests`). Its first run installs the test tools in **`requirements-dev.txt`** (pytest, and `praat-parselmouth` to check that Praat reads the TextGrids Lemmatrix writes; the ɛλicit page's own tests also need Playwright and skip without it); Lemmatrix itself doesn't install them (25 October, the audit). **Each run has settings of its own**, an .ini file in a temporary folder removed afterwards (`LEMMATRIX_SETTINGS_FILE`, read by `ui/prefs.settings_store`), so no run inherits another's settings and the real preferences are never touched. **`tests/conftest.py`** holds what the tests share: the sample database, the prototype tag structure, and the `app`, `make_win` and `win` fixtures (one copy; there were six).

### 3.1 General rules

- **Metadata:** stored as real elements, not XML comments. Elements survive saves, can be edited in a form and can be exported. Each database has **one language** and **one variety** (optional), each with a code, an **author** (owner), and three lists: **researchers** and **participants** (code, name, description; participants also a **consent file**, the document of informed consent, kept relative to the database's folder when it is inside it), and hierarchical **locations** (name, code, description). **Since 20 October** (for ɛλicit, IDEAS.md idea 19): researchers have an **email** (where a request's notice goes), and participants two checkboxes, **Identifiable?** and **Audio sharing?**, selected only when the consent form explicitly agrees to them. **Since 22 October** (format 8, IDEAS.md idea 44) researchers have **Identifiable?** too, selected for a new researcher (a participant's is deselected until the consent form says otherwise), and every checkbox is written outright, `identifiable="yes"` or `"no"` on every researcher and participant and `audio-sharing="yes"` or `"no"` on every participant, so the file reads the same without knowing the defaults. The checkboxes are part of the data: they say how it may be shared. **Who is named and whose recordings go in when the data is shared** follows them (the Ethical archive, section 3.15); the Anonymous archive names nobody. No contact details are kept for participants. Names are optional. Older files' single `<researcher>` is read as the author. **A new database starts with placeholders**, to be edited, **generic since 23 October** (Josh: "it is good to see the structure, but keep it generic"): researchers RS1 and RS2 (RS1 replaced by you when File → New database knows you, section 5.1), participants SP1 and SP2, and the places L1 › L1 Region › L1 Town and L2 › L2 Region › L2 Town (codes L1, L1R, L1T…, described as country, region and town). The places of 20 October, Canada › British Columbia › Ucluelet and Ethiopia › Gambella Regional State › Pade (not a known location; any resemblance is coincidental), stay only in the large test database (Test-300), as Easter eggs (`defaults.easter_egg_locations`). **The sample has invented metadata of its own** (Josh, 25 October; `make_sample.sample_metadata`): researcher **JJA** Joshua J Alexander (also the author), participant **DML** Dzosho Mofo Lakanoro (identifiable, audio sharing, so the Ethical archive has something to show), the places Canada › British Columbia › Langley and Wagandha › Whero State › Nowhero, and the funder University of Wagandha. **It has a text** (Josh, 25 October): T001 *The man and the lion*, Josh's recording with his TextGrid (tiers Phonetic, Phonemic, Pitch, Morphemes, Translation, Comment; the text's own tier mapping, main tier Phonetic), lines 3, 4 and 10 clipped as U001–U003 and linked to their lemmas (`make_sample.add_text`), and DML's invented signed consent form, `Documentation/DML consent form.pdf` (`make_sample.consent_form`). **Josh sets the sample up by hand** (25 October): he works in a fresh copy and copies `Sample.workspace`, `Sample.notebook` (questions, To Dos, snapshots, hypotheses) and `Whiteboard/` back into `samples/`; Help → Sample databases copies all of them with Documentation, and `make_sample.py` never writes over them or the recordings. **Typology** (format 10, 23 October; IDEAS.md ideas 9 and 56): a `<typology>` block right after the language and variety, one `<feature id value/>` each, always written, so a person reads it near the top and a machine finds it by one tag; the first feature is the **pitch kind** (`tone`: tonal, intonational, unspecified; section 3.5). It describes the language, so it is data, in both archives. **Acknowledgements** (format 12, 24 October; IDEAS.md idea 59): two lists in Edit → Metadata, each a small table with + and −: **Support** (organisation, grant or number, what it funded: funders, institutions, grants) and **Thanks** (person or group, what for), as `<acknowledgements>` after the wordlist (`<support organisation grant>what it funded</support>`, `<thanks who>what for</thanks>`); an empty row isn't kept. Support goes into both archives, with names replaced as in any free text (in the Ethical archive too since 25 October: the audit's P5). **The thanks only into an Ethical archive, and only when chosen there** (Josh: "The people being thanked personally aren't likely to have signed an agreement. - **Ids:** every entry and token has a short `id`. Notebook links point at it, so they survive changes to references and tags. Files without ids get them when opened; copies with the same id are given new ones.
- **Variety is database-level for now.** Comparing varieties (e.g. cognates across dialects) is left for later. If tokens ever get their own variety, a token without one takes the database's, so databases made now keep working.
- **Open on another computer:** while a database is open, Lemmatrix keeps a small hidden marker file next to it (`.<file name>.lemmatrix-lock`) with the computer, user, when it was opened and when Lemmatrix last checked in (every 5 minutes). OneDrive syncs it, so opening the same database on another computer warns, with **Open anyway** or **Don't open**. The marker goes when the database is closed. - **Elements and attributes:**
  - Elements hold linguistic content.
  - Attributes hold identity, tags, dates and file names.
  - Empty fields are not written.
- **Encoding:** UTF-8, with Unicode normalised (NFC). Characters that look identical then match in searches and file names.

### 3.2 Entries and tokens

- **Entry identity:** the `ref` attribute uniquely identifies each entry.
- **Entry fields:** plain text. **Lemma** is the main entry field. Lemma-level analysis is limited to the lemma list's CV shape filter (section 5.1).
- **Class fields** on every new database:
  - **Word Class** (built in): a closed field with its own list, hierarchical like any other (NOUN › Relational › Kinship). It doesn't restrict tags: it is what the tag groups' "usual for" hints refer to (section 3.3), which is why the field can be renamed but not deleted. Defaults: NOUN, VERB, PRONOUN, QUANT, ADJECTIVE, ADVERB.
  - **Stem type** (built in, 22 October, named so 23 October, Josh; `StemType`, `role="stem-type"`): what kind of stem a lemma is, a closed field like Word Class (renamable, not deletable). Its list starts **flat: simple, complex, loan** (it can be made hierarchical in Edit → Lists), and **a new lemma starts as simple**. A database from before gets it, empty, on opening, with a note in the status bar; one saved with the first version (named Origin, with root; complex › compound, derived, reduplicated; loan) is moved over, root becoming simple and the complex kinds complex.
  - **From** (22 October): beside Stem type, **one chip box** in the lemma dialogue (right under Stem type) for what the lemma comes from: **lemmas** it is built from and **languages** it was borrowed from, together. Its search finds lemmas by reference, form or gloss and **every ISO 639-3 language** (about 7,900, the codes Ethnologue uses; `lemmatrix/languages.py`, from `pycountry`) by name or code, the ~100 most widely spoken first; text that matches nothing is kept as a language name when Enter is pressed (a proto-language, a dialect). Click a chip to remove it. Stored on the entry as `from-lemmas="id id"` and `from-languages="arb; Proto-Nilotic"`. A lemma others are built from shows **Part of: …** under its From. The lemma list has a **From** column (shown as text, filterable) beside Stem type.
  - **Settings → General → Show Stem type and From** (this database, kept in the workspace): off hides the field, the box and both columns everywhere; what's filled in is kept.
  - **Tone Class** and **Inflection Class**: ordinary closed fields (below), renamable and deletable; in a new database they come **after Word Class and before Stem type** (Josh, 24 October; Edit → Fields reorders an existing database's). Their lists start as Tone Class H, L, LH, HL, and Inflection Class Nominal › I, II and Verbal › I, II (20 October; values are stored by name, so the word class tells a noun's I from a verb's I).
  - More class fields can be added.
- **Open and closed fields.** Every lemma or token field is one or the other, chosen when it's created:
  - **Open:** free text, filtered with "Filter for…" (section 5.2).
  - **Closed:** values come from a list kept in settings under the field's name (e.g. Tone Class: H, L, LH, HL). The list can be hierarchical and is edited like the tag structure. Closed fields are filtered with "Choose…". Word Class is closed. Tags count as closed.
- **Edit → Fields** adds, renames, deletes and reorders fields, in one dialogue with two separate lists, **Lemma fields** and **Token fields**; a field belongs to exactly one. A live **XML preview** beside the lists shows one example entry with one token, each field filled with dummy text named after it (e.g. `<ToneClass>ToneClass</ToneClass>`), updating as fields change.
- **Abbreviations and what a field answers to** (Josh, 26 October; IDEAS.md ideas 72 and 73; `lemmatrix/fieldkeys.py`): every field can have an **abbreviation** (Edit → Fields, up to 12 characters; stored as `abbr`, format 13). The token fields have them by default: **pht** Phonetic, **phm** Phonemic, **mph** Morphemes, **trs** Translation, **pch** Pitch, **com** Comment; other fields have none until given one. In a template (soundfile names, the Wordlist export) **a field answers to its name, its label and its abbreviation, in any case, with or without spaces**, so a field called Tone Class with the abbreviation tnc **reserves** {Tone Class}, {tone class}, {ToneClass}, {toneclass}… and {tnc}. **Edit → Fields warns, in red under the field, and won't accept** a name, label or abbreviation that would call another field (Josh: "a warning whenever someone tries to name or rename a field that would call another field"), or a word templates already use ({language}, {variety}, {speaker}, {reference}, {gloss}, {label}, {tags}, {date}, {title}) unless it is the field that word already means (the lemma's Gloss, a text's Title). Only what is new for a field is checked, so a database that already has a doubtful name still opens and saves. The lemma's and the token's Comment are one name on purpose and are no clash. **Labels and abbreviations may not hold `{ } >`** (the templates' own characters) **nor the characters Windows forbids in file names** (`/ \ : * ? " < > |`; idea 72), so any field can always be written in a template.
- **Settings → Display → Abbreviate field names in the edit dialogues** (this computer, off by default): the lemma, token, utterance and text dialogues label fields with their abbreviations (a field without one keeps its name), the full name in the hover. Column headers, Edit → Fields and the exports keep the full names.
- **The lemma dialogue's More arrow** (Josh, 24 October; IDEAS.md idea 60): the dialogue (narrower since 24 October: it opens at its content's width, at most 460 px) shows the category, the reference, **Lemma**, **Gloss** and **Word Class**; every other lemma field (Tone Class, Inflection Class, Stem type with From, Comment, Clarification, Taxon, the user's own) and *Gloss in file names* sit behind **More** (*More (2 filled)*: how many of them hold something), open or closed as last left on this computer, the way *Other languages* folds the second languages' glosses. **Edit → Fields → Under More in the lemma dialogue**, a checkbox for each lemma field (greyed for Lemma, Gloss and Word Class, always shown), changes which; kept in the workspace (`lemma_dialog/more`: how you work, not data); a new field starts under More. (Josh, from FLEx: a new entry felt like a field of empty fields, easy to click in the wrong place.)
- **Core fields can't be deleted** (19 October, IDEAS.md idea 9): Lemma, Gloss, Word Class, Phonetic, Phonemic, Morphemes, Pitch, Translation and Comment (on lemmas, tokens and texts), and texts' Title, Genre and Status, because other features depend on them. They can be renamed (their Label) and moved; Delete is greyed for them, with the reason in its tooltip. They're found by `role`; a Comment field without one (older files) gets `role="comment"` quietly when the database opens. A core field missing from a database (deleted before it was protected) comes back, empty, on opening, with a note.
  - Word Class is distinct from the reference categories in section 3.4.
- **Glosses:** any number per entry, each with a language (`lang`). Token translations work the same way.
- **Second languages** (20 October, for ɛλicit): the first gloss language (Edit → Metadata) is the primary one, the language of the analysis; the others are there to help speakers understand (Arabic in South Sudan, Amharic in Ethiopia), not for the analysis, so they are **hidden by default everywhere**: offered in the lemma list's, All data's, Utterances' and Bulk edit's Configure columns (`Gloss (ar)`, `Translation (ar)`, editable in place) and in Configure cell (`Translation (ar)`, under Translation), but never in the defaults; in the token and utterance dialogues they sit under a folded **Other languages (ar)** button, open or closed as last left (per computer); in the **lemma dialogue** (Josh, 24 October: the button was intrusive and in the wrong place) a small **+** in the theme colour at the right of the primary gloss shows the other languages' glosses right under it (− hides them), open or closed as last left on this computer, its hover saying how many are filled. Only the defaults changed: columns already set to show them stay. Spelling and predicted translations stay English only; Entire database export has every language, as before.
- **Token fields:**
  - Default: Phonetic (analysed), Phonemic (searchable), **Morphemes** (the morpheme glosses, the gloss line of IDEAS.md idea 2; not spell-checked), Pitch (pitch tools), Translation, Comment.
  - Built in on every token: tags, speaker, collection date, soundfile name.
  - Custom token fields can be added.

  - Speaker is searchable and filterable in All data, but not offered in the matrix views.

### 3.3 Tags and soundfile names

#### Tag structure (revised 18 October: tags no longer belong to word classes)

- **One customisable hierarchy**, set up in the tag structure editor (Edit → Lists → Tag structure), organised however the user likes: by word class (NOUN, VERB…), by broader categories (Nominal, Verbal), by construction or by frame.
  - **Groups** nest to any depth, e.g. Phonological → Following tone → before H, before L. Groups can also be flat, like Frames.
  - **Leaves:** tags.
- **Any tag can go on any token.** A possessed nominalised form can sit under a verb lemma with its possessor and case tags.
- **Tags under the same group exclude each other:** a token takes at most one tag from each lowest-level group. So:
  - 1SG.A + 3SG.O is allowed (Subject and Object are different groups), but 1SG.A + 3SG.A is not.
  - before H + after L is allowed (Following tone and Preceding tone are different groups), but before H + before L is not.
- **Tag names are unique in the whole structure** (tokens store the names). The editor shows clashes in red and won't accept them.
- **Each tag has a name and a file-name code.** Names may contain spaces ("before H"). Codes shared by two tags are shown in red, and OK asks before keeping them, since their file names couldn't be told apart.
- **Group defaults:** each group can mark **one** tag as its default (e.g. Simple in Stem, Affirmative in Polarity, isolation in Utterance position).
  - The default is a real tag: it is stored on the token, can be filtered on and shows in the matrix.
  - It has **no file-name code**, so it adds nothing to file names. Only a group's default can be without a code.
  - **The citation token always comes first** (Josh, 23 October: tokens can be a bit hodgepodge, but the citation form always comes first): a new tagless token goes in after the citation tokens at the start of its lemma (`Entry.add_token`; the token dialogue and Move token), any other at the end.
  - **Defaults are pre-selected on new tokens**, except the citation token, which stays tagless. A default is only added when its group is usual for the lemma (below) and the new token has no other tag from that group (e.g. a cell tagged Negative doesn't also get Affirmative). The user can unpick them.
  - The default defaults (20 October) are Singular (Number), Nominative (Case), Simple (Stem), Present (Tense), Perfective (Aspect), Indicative (Mood), Affirmative (Polarity) and isolation (Utterance position). Other groups have none.
- **Citation means no tags.** It isn't assumed to be singular, simple, affirmative or anything else. Since 20 October Singular is Number's default, so it has no file-name code: a token tagged only with defaults (Singular, Nominative…) would get the citation's file name, and is offered an Example tag like any clash. Tokens with Singular and something else (Singular + 1SG.POSS) are told apart by the other tag, as before.
- **A new default that would give two tokens one soundfile name** (Josh, 26 October; `lemmatrix/tagclash.py`, `ui/tagclash_dialog.py`): a group's default adds nothing to file names, so making a tag the default (Josh's case: Singular, SG, made Number's default) can give a token tagged with it and only other defaults the citation token's name. Until 26 October nothing said so (deleting a tag was checked, making one the default wasn't). Now OK in the tag structure lists the tokens that would share a name, with **Add Example tags** (each gets the next Example tag, x1…), **Choose for each…** or Cancel. Choose for each: per pair, *Add an Example tag* or *Merge into it*: the token with fewer tags (the citation token, often) stays and fills its empty fields from the other, which goes; with two recordings, *Merge, keeping …* names which one stays, the other waiting in Soundfiles › _Deleted recordings (the Recycle Bin on closing). Merging isn't offered where a field has two different values (nothing is lost). Links to a token that goes say it no longer exists; undo history is cleared, as for tag renames.
- **Everything is editable:** groups and tags can all be added (Add group at the top level, Add sub-group inside the selected one, Add tag), renamed, reordered and deleted. Renamed tags are followed in the tokens and saved matrices.
- **Tag order:** the order of groups and tags in the hierarchy is the order used everywhere: in the stored `tags` attribute, in displays and in file names.
- **Storage:** a token stores its tag names in one `tags` attribute, separated by comma and space, e.g. `tags="Plural, Genitive, before H"`, always written in tag order. Tag names therefore may not contain commas.
- **Changing a tag's code** (19 October): on OK, the recordings whose names it puts out of date are renamed straight away when names update automatically; otherwise Lemmatrix asks, **Rename** (only those recordings, one undo step, the same way as Update all soundfile names) or **Leave names out of date**.
- **Deleting a tag (or group) that tokens use** (19 October) says how many tokens use it and, on Yes, takes it off them when the dialogue is OK'd (their recordings' names follow, as for a code change). If taking it off would leave two tokens of a lemma with the same tags (so their recordings would get one name), it lists them and won't delete it until they're sorted out.
- Speaker and variety are not tags. Speaker comes from the participant list and the current session; variety comes from the database metadata.

#### "Usual for": hints, not rules (agreed 18 October)

- **A group can say which word classes it is usual for** (the Usual for column of the tag editor; double-click it, or Usual for…, to select classes in the Word Class list). Several classes are fine ("usual for NOUN and PRONOUN"). **No hint means usual for every lemma.** A subgroup takes its parent's hint unless it has its own; the column shows an inherited hint in grey brackets. A class includes those nested under it in the Word Class list.
- The hint lives on the group, so **renaming or moving a group keeps it**, and the top level doesn't have to match the word class names (Verbal can be usual for VERB). **Renaming a word class** in Edit → Lists updates the hints and the lemmas; a hint naming a class since deleted shows in red ("no longer in the Word Class list") until it's changed.
- **What the hint does:**
  - **Token dialogue:** the groups usual for the lemma's word class are shown, plus any group the token already has a tag in; **Show all tags (N more groups)** under them shows the rest, and stays on for Back and Next.
  - **Defaults:** only groups usual for the lemma give new tokens their default.
  - **Soft warning:** a tag from a group that isn't usual for the lemma is allowed, but marked: its chip has a dashed amber outline in All data, the matrices (in Lemma/Tag on the token itself, since a column spans many lemmas) and the token dialogue, its tooltip says why, the token dialogue has a note under the tags, and bulk edit's preview counts such tokens. The note and the preview have **Don't show this again**; **Settings → General → Warnings** turns it back on.
- **Changing a lemma's word class** changes nothing else: the tags stay, and any that are no longer usual are marked. (The tag conversion dialogue of 17 October was dropped with this change.)

#### Default tag structure

New databases start with this structure (revised 20 October). Everything in it can be added to, renamed, reordered or deleted, and **Edit → Lists → Tag structure → Reset to defaults** brings it back (section 3.8). The Word Class list starts with NOUN, VERB, PRONOUN, QUANT, ADJECTIVE and ADVERB.

| Top-level group (usual for) | Group | Tags (code); **default** |
|---|---|---|
| Nominal (NOUN) | Number | **Singular**, Plural (PL) |
| Nominal (NOUN) | Case | **Nominative**, Accusative (ACC), Ergative (ERG), Absolutive (ABS), Genitive (GEN), Pertensive (PER), Locative (LOC) |
| Nominal (NOUN) | Possession | 1SG.POSS, 2SG.POSS, 3SG.POSS, 1PL.POSS, 2PL.POSS, 3PL.POSS (codes as names) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Subject | 1SG.A, 1SG.S, 2SG.A, 2SG.S, 3SG.A, 3SG.S, 1PL.A, 1PL.S, 2PL.A, 2PL.S, 3PL.A, 3PL.S, NP.A, NP.S (codes as names; A the agent-like argument, S the single argument, NP a noun phrase) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Object | 1SG.O, 2SG.O, 3SG.O, 1PL.O, 2PL.O, 3PL.O, NP.O (codes as names; O the patient-like argument) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Stem | **Simple**, Infinitive (INF), Passive (PASS), Antipassive (AP), Causative (CAUS) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Tense | **Present**, Past (PST), Future (FUT) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Aspect | **Perfective**, Imperfective (IPFV) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Mood | **Indicative**, Imperative (IMP), Interrogative (INT) |
| Verbal (VERB) | Polarity | **Affirmative**, Negative (NEG) |
| Phonological (every lemma) | Following tone | before H (xH), before L (xL) |
| Phonological (every lemma) | Preceding tone | after H (Hx), after L (Lx) |
| Syntactic (every lemma) | Position vis predicate | preverbal (PREV), postverbal (POSTV), non-verbal predicate (PRED) |
| Syntactic (every lemma) | Utterance position | **isolation**, utterance-initial (INI), utterance-final (FIN) |
| Instances (every lemma) | Frames | Frame 1 (F1), Frame 2 (F2) |
| Instances (every lemma) | Examples | Example 1 (x1), Example 2 (x2) |

- **Defaults have no code** (a group's default adds nothing to file names), so Singular, Nominative, Present and Perfective are written without SG, NOM, PRS and PFV. Oblique (OBL) was in the prototype's Case group and is no longer a default.
- **Tag descriptions** (to do): each default tag and group is to get a one- or two-sentence description, with its source in the SIL Glossary of Linguistic Terms or Grammaticon (`lemmatrix/tagdefs.py`).
- **Environment codes** use x for the form itself: `xH` is "before H", `Hx` is "after H".
- **Instances** (18 October; Josh chose the name) is the **last group in the tag order**, so its codes always end the file name, e.g. `LXX-VXX_D001_goat_PL-xH-F2.wav`. Its tags sort as a second key, like frames always did: each form first on its own, then in its frames and examples.
  - **Frames** are the most basic way of comparing data: each frame is a fixed carrier context the forms are elicited in.
  - **Examples** tell apart tokens of a lemma with the same tags (another example of the same form), so their recordings get names of their own: `…_SG.wav`, `…_SG-x1.wav`. The codes are **lowercase** (Josh, 18 October: his convention for a non-linguistic gloss), as are his frames' (f1–f4).
- **Descriptions** (19 October): every tag and group can have one (the Description column of the tag editor; double-click to edit), e.g. a frame's carrier sentence ("I saw ___"). Stored as a `description` attribute on the `<tag>` or `<group>`. A tag's shows in its tooltip wherever it appears (the token dialogue, All data's and Utterances' Tags column, the matrix headers, Configure matrix's tag picker, Bulk edit's tag list), a group's on its name in the token dialogue. **File → Export → Tag structure as CSV…** lists every group and tag with its code, default, "usual for" and description.
- **Known limit:** S and A subjects share one group, so a token can't have two subjects. Nothing stops an intransitive subject (e.g. 1SG.S) being combined with an object, though. That's left to the user for now.

#### Soundfile names

- **The pattern is fully customisable** in settings. The default:

  `{language}-{variety}_{reference}_{gloss}_{tags}.wav`

  `{speaker}` is no longer in the default pattern but is still available as a placeholder for anyone who wants it.

  - **Empty parts drop their separator.** A dash or underscore is written only if the part next to it has a value, so a database with no variety gets `LXX_D001_goat.wav`, not `LXX-_D001_goat.wav`.
  - **`{tags}`** is all of the token's tag codes joined by the tag separator (default `-`), in tag order. `GEN, PL` and `PL, GEN` therefore give the same name. Group defaults add nothing.
  - **`{gloss}`** uses the entry's `file-gloss`, which is copied from the primary gloss when the entry is created. Later gloss edits don't change it; it can be changed deliberately.
  - **Spaces and punctuation are written as Praat writes them** (19 October, replacing "spaces stay as spaces"): Praat turns spaces and ASCII punctuation other than `+ - = _` into underscores, and its Save suggests that name, so an edited `…_to run.wav` was saved as a second file `…_to_run.wav`. All file names now follow Praat's: `to run` gives `LXX_V012_to_run.wav`, a tag code `1SG.A` gives `…_1SG_A.wav`; the gloss, label and codes themselves keep their spaces and dots. Tokens', utterances' and texts' files alike. On opening, older names are renamed once (whatever the Update names setting); if Praat had already saved its own newer copy beside one, that copy is used and the old one held like a deleted recording; if the old one is newer, both are left and listed. Closing without saving puts everything back.
  - Other placeholders available for custom patterns: `{date}` (collection date), and **any entry or token field** by its name, label or abbreviation, in any case, with or without spaces: `{Word Class}`, `{wordclass}`, `{phm}` (section 3.2). The template's own words come first, so `{gloss}` is still the file gloss.

  - **A letter and three digits**, in the list's order with no gaps. A lemma of one's own in an EACW database takes the number after the list's last in its category: `suggest_reference` skips every number the EACW has, its hidden items included, so a new lemma never takes a hidden item's place.

### 3.5 Pitch

- **Storage:** digits `0`–`9`, colons, spaces and commas, as typed. Brackets or tone letters are added for display only.
- **Notation (revised 16 October):**
  - **Spaces separate syllables**; a **comma** marks a pause (not necessarily a word boundary).
  - **Digits are pitch points.** A syllable needs at least two points.
  - **Between two adjacent points is one short step. Each colon between them adds one step:** no colon = short, `:` = long, `::` = extra long.
  - Examples:
    - `11` short level low; `1:1` long level low; `1::1` extra-long level low
    - `14` short (quick) rise; `1:4` long steady rise
    - `42` quick fall; `4:2` long fall; `4:42` long level high, then a quick fall; `42:1` quick fall, then a long fall
    - `142` short rise-fall; `1:42` long rise, then a quick fall
  - **A lone digit is an extra-short level** (Josh, 21 October): `5` stays `5`, drawn as a level half a short step long and played as a brief tone (`pitch.EXTRA_SHORT`). Until 21 October a lone digit was doubled (`5` became `55`); data saved that way stays as it is.
  - Colons at the start or end of a syllable mean nothing and are flagged.
- **Drawing lengths:** a short step is drawn about as long as an underscore `_` in the field's font, a long step as two (`__`), an extra-long step as three. **Syllables are about one underscore apart; a comma makes the gap about two (`__`).**
- **Pitch playback:** a token's pitch can be played as an abstract sound, in one of three voices chosen in Settings → Pitch: **Buzz** (a robotic buzz following the trace exactly), **Hum** (a human-like "mmm mmm mmm") or **Whistle** (a human-like whistle; the default since 19 October). Every short step lasts the same time, colons lengthen steps as in the drawing, syllables are separated by a short silence and a comma by a longer one. Levels are two semitones apart (a wide display range is squeezed into about an octave and a half). The end fades out gently (the last 150 ms) and 360 ms of silence follow, so playback ends without a click (Josh's pick of three versions, 18 October).
- **Pitch display range** (setting, default `1-5`):
  - Sets which digit is drawn lowest and which highest; everything in between is spaced evenly. `1-5` matches Unicode tone letters and keeps typing on the near side of the keyboard.
  - Can be set to anything from 0 to 9, e.g. `0-9` or `1-7` for sentence-length tokens. Changing it redraws all pitch displays.
  - A digit outside the range is drawn beyond the edge and flagged, rather than silently stretching the scale.
- **Display:**
  - **Default: contours drawn by the program.** Works with any range; a comma is drawn as a gap.
  - **Alternative (later): Unicode tone letters** with the staves hidden (Charis SIL `cv92`). Only available when the range is five levels or fewer.
  - The stored data stays as typed either way.
- **Checking:** once syllabification exists, the program compares the number of pitch groups with the syllables in Phonetic and flags mismatches. Commas aren't counted as syllables.
- **Hiding the pitch system** (IDEAS.md idea 9, designed and built 23 October; `lemmatrix/pitchview.py`): **Settings → Pitch → Hide the pitch system** (deselected by default; until 26 October it was *Show the pitch system*, selected to show: Josh turned it round, so selecting it hides, and while it is selected the *This database* and *This computer* boxes under it grey out whole, headings and labels too, a bigger cue that something is off) and **View → Hide pitch system** (selected while hidden; no shortcut; *Show pitch system*, selected while shown, until 26 October, when Josh made it Hide to match Settings). It is a window beside the main one, not modal (Josh, 22 October): the main window stays usable while it is open (e.g. to look at the token a question is about), it can be minimised, opening it again brings the same window forward, and it fills again from the notebook whenever it becomes active. View → To Do is the same.
- **How a link reads** (its preview in Cogitate, the notebook's lists, Questions and To Do; Josh, 25 October; `notebook.describe`): **{reference} {form} {meaning} · {tags}**. A question in Cogitate reads **Q: {its text}**. The lists show a link as it reads now (the lemma may have changed since it was pushed), and what was written then when the lemma or token is gone. ɛλicit's request list keeps its own look (*D001 gatto · Plural*; Josh). The Wordlist export's form is a template of its own (section 3.16).

A **snapshot** is the main window's complete setup when it was taken (section 3.14, Snapshots), an item of the day it was taken (`notebook.Snapshot`: an id, a name, the day and time, how the setup read, the setup itself, pinned or not). **Taken** with the camera (a click; section 3.14). **View → Snapshots** (**Ctrl+Shift+P**, and the 48 px Snapshots button between the ? and the To Do flag, section 5.1): every snapshot, newest first, the pinned ones on top, a window beside the main one like View → Questions; Session, Snapshot (its name), Time, Setup (left blank while the name is still the setup's description), a search, and **Restore** (also a double-click: the view comes back in the main window), **Rename**, **Pin to the top / Unpin**, **Link to hypothesis…**, **Delete** and **Open in notebook**, the same on right-click. The notebook's **Snapshots** tab (after To Do) is the day's list with the same buttons. A snapshot is named as its setup reads ("Lemma/Tag · Basic Imperatives · 155 lemmas · filtered") until renamed. Taking, renaming, pinning and deleting are each **one undo step** (`Document.change_snapshots`, `SnapshotCommand`); a snapshot taken writes "Snapshot taken 10:15: …" under **=====SNAPSHOT HISTORY=====** (History sections, above), which undo takes out again. **Deleting never breaks a hypothesis:** a link to a snapshot keeps its own copy of the setup (section 3.14), so it still restores the view; it is shown grey, *Snapshot deleted from the list*, not red. Snapshots are the researcher's own work, like the rest of the notebook: in every Full backup, in an archive only when the notebook is selected in, their names and setups then scrubbed like the rest (section 3.15).
- **Pinned questions and To Dos** (Josh, 22 October; IDEAS.md idea 49): a **pushpin** (in the theme colour) button and a right-click **Pin to the top / Unpin** on the notebook's Questions and To Do tabs and in View → Questions and View → To Do. In the notebook's tabs a pinned item is at the top of **its own day**; the two windows show every pinned one at the top. **Resolving unpins it** (Mark as open doesn't pin it again). Stored on the item in the notebook (`pinned="yes"`), so it travels with the database.

### 3.9 The main folder

*Agreed 18 October (IDEAS.md idea 7) and built the same day as its stage 1.* The code is `lemmatrix/layout.py`.

- **One main folder per database** (any name). Lemmatrix assumes the layout inside it instead of keeping paths in Settings:

  ```
  Main folder/
  ├─ <database>.xml          (the data)
  ├─ <database>.notebook     (the researcher's own work: notes, questions, hypotheses; section 3.7)
  ├─ <database>.workspace    (working state: saved matrices, filters, the session…; section 3.13)
  ├─ Soundfiles/             (the data's recordings and TextGrids)
  │  ├─ Tokens/              (lemma tokens)
  │  ├─ Utterances/          (utterances' recordings, section 3.10)
  │  ├─ Texts/               (texts' recordings and TextGrids, section 3.11)
  │  ├─ Segments/            (the segment scratchpad's recordings, section 5.1; made with the first)
  │  └─ _Deleted recordings/
  ├─ Backups/                (the automatic local backups)
  ├─ Documentation/          (consent forms and other documentation)
  ├─ Images/                 (pictures for ɛλicit requests, 20 October)
  ├─ Elicit/                 (ɛλicit requests and the recordings that came back, section 5.12)
  ├─ Whiteboard/             (drawings saved from Tools → Whiteboard, section 5.14; made with the first)
  └─ Exports/                (the default place File → Export offers)
  ```

  - A **new database** starts with Soundfiles/Tokens, Documentation, Images and Exports (Backups comes with the first save).
  - **The data and the researcher's own work** (section 1): the database and Soundfiles are the data; Documentation holds the consent forms, which say how it may be shared and travel only in a password-protected zip; the notebook, the workspace, Elicit's requests, the whiteboard's drawings, Backups and Exports, and later `Papers/` and the analyses, are not archive data. **File → Full backup** takes everything; the **archives** take only the data (section 3.15).
  - **Images** (20 October, IDEAS.md idea 19): a picture belongs to a token by its name alone, the reference plus the token's tag codes as in soundfile names, written as Praat would name it: `D001.jpg` for the citation token, `D001_PL.jpg` for the plural, `D001_PL-1SG_POSS.gif` for "my goats" (JPG, PNG or animated GIF, any case). **No falling back** on the lemma's picture (one goat is not three). Never shown in Lemmatrix and never in the database (`filenames.image_stem`, `find_image`); **Edit → Metadata → Open images folder** opens it. The full backup includes it; the archives don't.
  - **Segments** (24 October, IDEAS.md idea 46): the phonetics scratchpad's own recordings of sequences, found by their names alone (like Images: nothing in the database file), `{language}-{variety}-{speaker}_{sequence}.wav` (`LXX-VXX-SP1_ʌ.wav`; with no speaker `LXX-VXX_ʌ.wav`). A participant's are data (the Ethical archive takes them with Audio sharing?); a researcher's survive a Full backup but go into no archive (Josh); one with no speaker goes into no archive. A person's code renamed in Edit → Lists renames their segment soundfiles. Lemmatrix's own IPA soundfiles are the program's (`lemmatrix/ipa/`), never copied into a database (section 5.1).
  - **Settings → Sound has no sound folder** any more.
  - **Deleted tokens' recordings** wait in `Soundfiles/_Deleted recordings`; one left in the old place (inside the sound folder) is still put back or emptied.
  - **Exports** is only the default: CSV exports and the archives start there, and can be saved anywhere.
- **Consent forms:** a consent link is relative to the main folder when the form is inside it. A form that isn't where its link says is looked for by file name in Documentation (subfolders too) and relinked quietly when the database opens (a status-bar note; saving keeps the new link).
- **One database per main folder:** two in one folder would share Soundfiles, so opening a database with another Lemmatrix database beside it warns. **New database** in a folder that already has one offers to make a folder named after the new database for it.
- **No Save as** (for now), and so no warning about moving soundfiles. A database never saved (rare) asks where to go when it is first saved, with the same check.
- **File → Rename database…** changes the database's name (in the title, Edit → Metadata) and its file name together; the file name follows the name (`LXX-VXX_Complete_Lexicon` → `LXX-VXX_Main_Database`) until it is edited itself. The notebook, the workspace and the automatic backups in Backups are renamed with it (all or nothing; a name already taken is refused), the window's settings move to the new name, and the database is saved under it. Not while it is open on another computer. Renaming the XML by hand loses the notebook's link (it is found by name) and these settings.
- **File → Full backup…** zips the whole main folder with its subfolders (the open-database marker and half-finished writes left out: `.saving`, `.part`, `.lemmatrix-writing`; a recording caught in the middle of a rename, `.lemmatrix-renaming`, is kept, since after a crash it may be the only copy) as `<database> YYYY-MM-DD.zip`, e.g. `LXX-VXX_Complete_Lexicon 2026-10-18.zip`, with the top folder's name inside the zip. Unsaved changes are offered a save first. The save dialogue starts in the **full backup folder** (Settings → General; Documents if none, or the folder of the first backup); a full backup can never go inside the main folder. Recordings are stored as they are (they hardly compress) and everything else compressed; the zip is written under a temporary name, so a cancelled or failed backup leaves nothing. Lemmatrix doesn't delete old full backups.
- **Weekly reminder** (per computer): when the last full backup is 7 or more days old (with none yet: a week after the database was first opened with this version), opening the database shows a message with **Back up now** and **Thanks, remind me again in 7 days**.

### 3.10 Utterances (format 5)

*Agreed 18 October (IDEAS.md idea 7) and built the same day as its stage 2.* The window and dialogue are `ui/utterances.py`.

- **An utterance is a token without a lemma:** one reference, one token (one transcription, one recording), with the usual token fields (Morphemes included), tags, speaker, date and file, an optional **file label** (like a lemma's `file-gloss`, but editable at any time) and **links to lemmas** (by id). Related utterances are found by searching tags or comments. In the model, `Utterance` is an `Entry` with exactly one token, kept in `db.utterances`, so undo, comments, the notebook and backups treat it like an entry.
- **Categories:** the reference's letter is the category. U (Unorganised) and W, X, Y, Z (named "Own category W"… until renamed) are in the reference categories list, marked as for utterances. **Edit → Lists → Reference categories** edits the whole list (letter, name, for lemmas or utterances, automatic or by hand); a letter in use can't change, be removed or switch between lemmas and utterances, and no letter may start another (W and WX, say: WX01 couldn't be told from a W reference). The lemma dialogue offers lemma categories only. References go past 999 (W1000 follows W999) with nothing renamed. **Move to another category…** (right-click) gives the next free reference there and renames the recording, after asking.
- **File names** have a pattern of their own (Settings → Sound → Utterance file names, default `{language}-{variety}_{reference}_{label}`; it needs `{reference}`) and no tags: `LXX-VXX_W001_lion_house.wav`, in `Soundfiles/Utterances`. Changing the label renames the recording (when names update automatically), with undo; Update all soundfile names covers utterances too.
- **Three ways to make one:**
  - **Add:** pick the category; the utterance dialogue opens on a new utterance, which is added when it's saved with anything in it (or recorded).
  - **Import…** or **dragging WAVs** onto the grid: pick the category; each file is copied into `Soundfiles/Utterances` under its utterance's name, with the file's name as the label (underscores as spaces, characters forbidden in file names left out) and the session's speaker and date; the originals stay as they are. One undo step for the whole import (undo moves the copies aside, like deleted recordings). A WAV already in `Soundfiles/Utterances` is renamed there, not copied. **A TextGrid beside the WAV** (same name) is read with the tier mapping and fills in the fields (it isn't copied, and there is no source link); with several lines Lemmatrix asks: Join the lines, Pick one…, Add as a text (the recording and TextGrid become a text instead) or Skip this file.
  - **Clip as utterance** in the Texts tab (section 3.11).
- **Tags:** lemma tags can go on utterances. A tag group can be marked **utterances only** (in the tag editor's Usual for… dialogue; the Usual for column then says "utterances only", inherited by sub-groups): a rule, not a hint. Its tags are never offered on lemma tokens, not even under Show all tags, and one put there another way is marked like an unusual tag ("for utterances only"). **New utterances start with no tags** (no word class to take defaults from), and nothing on an utterance is marked unusual.
- **View → Texts and utterances** (Ctrl+U): a window of its own, **laid out like the main window's Matrix pane** (Josh, 18 October: nothing to look for anew): the title in the theme colour with the **Texts** and **Utterances** tabs on the right of its row; the grid; an options row under it (Auto-play token soundfile, the same option as the main window's); and the options panel at the bottom, below a splitter, with the buttons, the quick search and the count line ("3 of 3 utterances shown"). Its size, place and splitters are remembered as they are adjusted (20 October). The Texts tab is section 3.11.
  - **Utterances tab:** a grid like All data (clicking an utterance plays it with Auto-play on): Reference, File label, Tags, Pitch drawing, the token fields, Lemmas, Speaker, Date, Soundfile (Present / Missing / Rerecord), and Category in Configure columns; editable in place (token fields and the file label), the same column filters, quick search, Clear all filters. Add, Import…, Delete, Edit, Play, Configure columns; Delete, Enter, Space, Ctrl+Space and **Ctrl+Shift+Space** (play the pitch; Josh, 18 October) as in All data; a right-click menu with Play pitch and Move to another category…. Deleting holds the recording for undo, as for tokens.
  - **The utterance cell** (IDEAS.md idea 22, Josh, 21 October): at the right of the Utterances tab's options panel, the **selected utterance as a cell** in the text lines' style: the same Configure view (which fields, their order, which line up; one setting with the lines), the reference and file label as its title, and the **Comment box under the fields** (Josh, 21 October: as wide as the cell, taking the room the fields leave; if even two lines of it wouldn't fit, the options area is pulled up), with Push to question. Its width is adjustable with the splitter, **at most half of the options area** (remembered on this computer). Over it: the pencil (**Edit mode**, also Ctrl+E on the Utterances tab), **Configure view** (the Configure cell icon, as in the matrices, since it configures the cell, 25 October: the same setting as a text's lines, not one of its own) and /[β]/. Edit mode edits fields in place, each edit an undo step as in the grid; Ctrl + mouse wheel zooms it; highlighted words give **Create token** (right-click), as in a text's lines; the /[β]/ button turns the display conventions on or off. No bookmarks there.
- **The utterance dialogue** is the token dialogue (Ctrl+Space and Ctrl+Shift+Space play from inside any of its fields, the file label, Lemmas search and Date included, as in the token dialogue) with: a header with the reference, the category's name and the editable file label (`W001 · Clause types · hare ran`); the utterance-only groups and the groups with no "usual for" hint first, Show all tags for the rest; no predicted translations (they are learned from lemmas' glosses); a **Lemmas** box (type a reference, form or gloss, pick from the list; click a chip to remove the link); the recording panel as for tokens; Back and Next through the grid's rows.
- **Elsewhere:** the notebook's Recordings tab lists the day's utterances, marked as utterances; utterance comments count as the day's comments and Push to question works, with links that open the utterance in the window. Renamed participants, field values and token fields are followed in utterances; the archives copy utterance recordings into `Soundfiles/Utterances` (the Ethical archive only, when the speaker agreed to audio sharing) and replace names in their comments and translations (section 3.15).
- **Clipped utterances** show their **Source** in the dialogue: the text's reference and title and the line's times, **Play in context** (the line with 2 s either side, from the text's recording) and **Show the text** (the Texts tab, with the line selected).
- **In the tools** (19 October): Check spelling checks utterances' English translations (and the Utterances grid's Translation column is underlined); File → Export → Entire database has a row for each utterance after the lemmas, with a **Kind** column (Lemma or Utterance; only when the database has utterances), the reference, and the file label in Gloss in file names; Find missing or unlinked recordings looks in `Soundfiles/Utterances`. **Not yet:** Bulk edit.

### 3.11 Texts and songs (format 6)

*Agreed 18 October (IDEAS.md idea 7) and built the same evening as its stage 3.* The tab and the text dialogue are `ui/texts.py`; reading TextGrids is `lemmatrix/textgrid.py`, with `praatio` (MIT licence).

- **A text (T001) or song (S001) is a catalogue record:** reference, the **text fields** (Edit → Fields has a third list, Text fields: Title, Genre, Transcription status, Description and Comment by default, and any of the user's own), date, participants, researchers and locations (codes that follow renames in Edit → Lists), the recording and its TextGrid. **Genre** (narrative › personal narrative, folktale, history; procedural; hortatory; expository; conversation; song; prayer; elicited; other) and **Transcription status** (untranscribed, transcribed, translated, analyzed, finished; 20 October) are closed lists in Edit → Lists. T (Texts) and S (Songs) are reference categories marked for texts.
- **Adding a text** (Add): the text dialogue (600 px wide at first, resizable, its size remembered; file names shown without their folders, the full path in the tooltip), with Choose recording… (a TextGrid of the same name beside it is picked too) and Choose TextGrid… (both open in `Soundfiles/Texts`, or beside the recording already chosen). On OK both are **copied** into `Soundfiles/Texts` under `{language}-{variety}_{reference}_{title}` (Settings → Sound → Text file names), the originals left as they are; one undo step, and undo moves the copies aside. A file that is **already in `Soundfiles/Texts`** is renamed there, not copied (undo, or discarding the changes, gives it its old name back). The title in the names is a **file label** copied when the text is added; editing the title later renames nothing, but **Title in file names** can be changed deliberately, and then both files are renamed (with undo). Update all soundfile names covers texts' files too.
- **The lines stay in Praat.** Lemmatrix reads them from the TextGrid when a text is chosen (again only when the file has changed) and never writes it.
- **Stretches with no main-tier text** (Josh, 21 October: much easier on import): where only other mapped tiers have text (a translation tier filled, the main tier's segment missing or empty), the stretch is a line too (its other tiers' segments, overlapping ones together), flagged in red beside its times: "no main-tier segment". Typing its main field (edit mode) writes a segment into the main tier there, and it becomes an ordinary line.
- **Tier mapping:** a database-level list of rules, tier name → token field (Phonetic, Phonemic, Morphemes, Translation with its language, Comment…) or Ignore, and which tier is the **main tier** (its intervals with text are the lines; the other tiers' text is matched to them by overlapping time, joined with spaces). Defaults: tx → Phonetic (main), ge → Morphemes, ft → Translation (en), mb → Ignore. **Speakers:** a tier named `tx@SP1` counts as tx, said by SP1, and is matched only with SP1's other tiers (and tiers with no speaker); without a speaker in the names, a text with one participant gives that participant. The text dialogue lists the TextGrid's tiers with what each fills in and a Main checkbox, and counts the lines: a tier with no rule yet is **learned** for the next texts; a choice that differs from the database's rules becomes **the text's own mapping**. **Edit → Lists → Tier mapping** edits the database's rules. Renamed token fields are followed in the rules.
- **The Texts tab**, laid out like the rest of the window: the texts on top (Reference, Title, Genre, Transcription status, Date, Participants, Utterances clipped from it, Recording; Researchers, Locations, TextGrid and the other fields in Configure columns; the usual filters and search); below, **the chosen text's lines** (Start, End, Speaker, a column for each mapped field, and the utterances clipped from each line). **Each list has its controls right under it** (Josh, 18 October): under the texts, a panel with Add, Delete, Edit, Configure columns, Clear all filters, the texts' search and their count; under the lines, the window's options panel with **Play line** (also Ctrl+Space, Space, Enter, double-click), **Play in context**, **Clip as utterance…** and **Open in Praat**, the lines' search and their count. Double-clicking a line's Utterances shows that utterance. The window **opens on the Texts tab** (Josh, 18 October), except when it opens to show an utterance.
- **Clip as utterance…** (one line or several, one undo step): pick the utterance category; each line becomes an utterance with its fields from the mapped tiers, the line's speaker (or the text's only participant) and the text's date, a label from the first six words of its translation, and its stretch of the text's recording **cut into a file of its own** in `Soundfiles/Utterances`, with **100 ms either side** (Settings → Sound → Clips keep either side), at the recording's own sample rate and bit depth, the speaker and date in its metadata. The utterance keeps a **link to its line**: the text, the line's times, what was copied from each tier and when the TextGrid was last changed (for Pull, stage 4).
- **Texts' files are named the way Praat names objects** (18 October; tokens' and utterances' too since 19 October, section 4): Praat turns spaces and ASCII punctuation other than `+ - = _` into underscores in an object's name (checked against Praat 6.1), and its Save suggests that name, so `…_Hyena and Hare.TextGrid` was saved by Praat as a new file `…_Hyena_and_Hare.TextGrid` and Lemmatrix never saw the edit. Texts' recordings and TextGrids are now named that way (`LXX-VXX_T001_Hyena_and_Hare.TextGrid`; the title itself keeps its spaces), so Praat's Save replaces the file Lemmatrix reads. On opening, older names are renamed once; if Praat had already saved its own newer copy, that copy is used and the old one held like a deleted recording (closing without saving puts everything back). A test runs the whole round with Praat itself (praat-parselmouth, when installed).
- **Open in Praat** opens the text's recording **with its TextGrid** in one editor, **zoomed to the selected line** (1 s either side) with the line selected (the script's `editor: grid` → `Zoom:` and `Select:`). **Ctrl+Alt+Space** does the same, as in All data (Josh, 24 October); on the Utterances tab it opens the selected utterance's recording.
- **Deleting a text** holds its recording and TextGrid for undo, like deleted recordings; utterances clipped from it stay, with their own recordings. **Several texts** can be selected (Ctrl+click, Shift+click, Ctrl+A; Josh, 24 October) and deleted together, one question and one undo step; the lines shown are the current text's. **The Utterances grid** selects the same way: Delete (or right-click **Delete N utterances**) deletes the selected ones as one undo step, and the right-click menu offers **Add N to the ɛλicit request**; Pull, Push and Unlink already worked on every selected utterance. **Not in Cogitate, Questions or To Do** (Josh: hypotheses are crucial to protect): they stay one at a time.
- **Notebook:** a **Texts** tab lists the texts **worked on** that day (Josh, 21 October: the day its lines are edited or clipped, not the day it was added): the first line edit or clip of a day records the text in that day's session (`<text id="…"/>` in the notebook file; the day gets a session if it has none, with the Current Session's people and places). Undoing the edit leaves it listed. Double-click opens one in the Texts tab.
- **Pull** (stage 4, `lemmatrix/pull.py`; the dialogues are `ui/pull_dialog.py`). A clipped utterance's line is found again (the line with the same times, or else the one that overlaps it most, among the same speaker's tiers) and compared with **what was copied**, not with the utterance as it is now, so edits made in Lemmatrix never count as a change. A TextGrid not saved since the clip isn't read at all. States: the same; **changed** (a tier's text, an emptied interval included); **times moved** (the same text); **line not found**; **TextGrid missing** (or unreadable); text deleted (shown, not flagged). A tier gone from the TextGrid altogether (renamed?) changes nothing.
  - **Flags:** the reference is red, with the reason in its tooltip, and the **Source** column (`T001 0:00.50–0:01.50 – changed`) can be filtered for "Changed in the TextGrid". The window checks again whenever it becomes active (back from Praat), and the Texts tab shows the lines again if the TextGrid changed. (Until 21 October the lines were only read again if the database had utterances, so in a database without any, a segment filled in Praat never showed up.)
  - **Pull from source…** (right-click, the Source box in the utterance dialogue, Tools → Check clipped utterances…): a dialogue lists each field it will replace, as it is in the utterance and in the TextGrid now (and the times, if they moved); only the fields filled from the tiers that changed are replaced, and the line is remembered as it is now. One undo step for any number. If the times moved, **cut the clip again** is offered (on by default): the new clip has the same name and padding, and the old recording goes to the Recycle Bin (undo can't bring it back, as with a replaced recording).
  - **Unlink from the text…** forgets the line (for one that's gone): the utterance keeps its fields and recording. With undo.
  - **Tools → Check clipped utterances…** lists every flagged one and every one edited here, with Pull… and Push… (the selected ones, or all that can be), Unlink… and Show.
- **Push to source** (agreed with Josh, 18 October): the other direction. A field **edited in Lemmatrix** (it differs from what was copied or last pulled; spacing aside) marks the utterance **"edited here"** in the Source column (not red: it isn't a problem) and in the Source box. **Push to source…** (right-click, the Source box, Check clipped utterances) lists each edited field with its tier, as it is in the TextGrid now and as it will become, each with a **checkbox**; a deselected one stays "edited here". Where the line spans **several segments** of a tier (a word-level tier), they are **merged** into one segment with the whole field, with the warning "This push will merge several segments in Praat." The line's **exact** boundaries are read from the TextGrid (the stored ones are rounded to 0.1 ms, and a rounded start once reached into the empty segment before the line, so the merge swallowed the empty segments either side: fixed 19 October); a segment counts only if more than 0.5 ms of it is inside the line, and an empty segment reaching past the line is split at the line's edge, so a push never moves a boundary outside the line. When the line was **changed in Praat** as well, its differing fields are listed too, but **deselected** and marked "changed in Praat" (selecting writes Lemmatrix's version back over Praat's), so either direction can be chosen, e.g. after restoring a TextGrid from Backups. Before writing, the TextGrid is copied into **Backups** (`<name>_<date>_<time>.TextGrid`), since undo can't reach it; the dialogue says to **close the TextGrid in Praat first** (Praat keeps its own copy, and saving it there would undo the push), and offers to open it in Praat afterwards at the line. One write per text; the utterances' links are updated as one undo step. A change made in Praat to another tier and not yet pulled stays flagged.
- **Archives** (section 3.15): a text's TextGrid goes into both archives, since it is text and not a voice, with names replaced in its tiers' text and, in the Anonymous archive, the codes in its tier names renumbered (`tx@KDB` → `tx@SP1`); its recording only into the Ethical archive, and only when every participant of the text agreed to audio sharing. Participants' names in the texts' open fields and file labels are replaced like the rest. TextGrids can still hold other identifying things, so the report's reminder applies to them too.

### 3.12 Editing a text's lines (agreed 19 October)

*Josh: "I hate working the TextGrid in Praat and would rather set up the utterance boundaries once, then work in Lemmatrix." Built the same night.* The view is `ui/lines_view.py`; editing a TextGrid is `lemmatrix/lines.py`; the glossing rules are `lemmatrix/interlinear.py`.

Not saving leaves the files as they were. If Praat saved a TextGrid after edits were made here, saving asks: *Save my edits* (Praat's version goes to Backups), *Keep Praat's* (the edits here are dropped), or Cancel. Adding a text records the comments already in its TextGrid in the day the text was recorded.

### 3.13 The workspace file (format 7)

*Agreed and built 21 October (IDEAS.md idea 36).* `lemmatrix/workspace.py` (no Qt); the window's part is in `ui/document.py` and `ui/main_window.py`.

- **Why:** the goal is *human readable, machine processable, ethically collected and shared linguistic data*, so the database keeps only what describes the data or is needed to read it. **What you are working on** goes in `<database>.workspace`, beside the database and its notebook; **how it looks** stays on the computer (Josh: "visual layout is all about where you are working from, filters is what you are working on"). The workspace is the researcher's own (section 1): it goes into the Full backup, and both archives write a blank one.

A column's width, once set, stays while Lemmatrix is open, even when the columns are set again (Configure columns, Settings, Edit → Metadata: closing Metadata used to size All data's columns to their contents again; fixed 24 October); only a column newly shown is sized to its data.
- **The file:** XML (`<workspace format="1">`): `<settings>`, `<matrices>`, `<rerecord>` (each `<token id>` with its reference and tags as a readable label), `<spelling>`, and `<state>` with one `<value name>` per item, its value as JSON text. **One workspace for everyone** for now (split per researcher if databases are ever shared).
- **Written as soon as something changes** (0.4 s after the last change; on switching databases and closing), never marking the database unsaved, atomically like the notebook; nothing is written when nothing changed. **Saving the database** puts a copy of the workspace into Backups first (kept like the database's), then writes it. An **unreadable** workspace is never written over: the database opens with the defaults, after a message.

The XML viewer shows the database only (Josh: it is there to show the data is human readable, not for workspace settings).

### 3.14 Cogitate: hypotheses (notebook format 3)

*Designed with Josh and built 21 October (IDEAS.md idea 35; snapshots, idea 16, settled with it).* The hypotheses are `lemmatrix/hypotheses.py` (no Qt); the window and the link picker are `ui/cogitate.py`; undo is `HypothesisCommand` in `ui/document.py`.

- **Whose they are** (Josh, 22 October): hypotheses are the researcher's unpublished thinking, and the evidence linked to them is the researcher's analysis: the most private part of the notebook. That is why they live in the notebook and never in the database: the links point at the data, the data never points back. They are in every Full backup and in no archive unless the researcher selects the notebook in (section 3.15). A reader of the archived data meets the data, not the hypotheses about it.

- **What a hypothesis is:** a running idea, modified slowly over time, kept in the **notebook** at its end (`<hypotheses>`). It has a number, **H1, H2…** (never reused, like references; gaps stay when one is deleted), a **summary** (usually a one-sentence claim), a **careful formulation**, a **confidence** (from the Confidence list in Edit → Lists, by default **initial**, **developing**, **proven**, **dubious**; kept with the hypotheses in the notebook, so the database format didn't change), its **links**, and the days it was made and last edited (the session's day).
- **Links**, stored with the hypothesis, never on the item, by id: a **token**, an **utterance**, a **text's line** (the text's id and the line's start time, with the line's text copied; a boundary moved in Praat goes to the nearest line, as bookmarks do), a **snapshot** (below), a **question** or **To Do** (by its id; found again after a Convert), a **comment** (the notebook's record of it, copied as it was that day; it goes to its token or line), **another hypothesis**, and a **whiteboard drawing** (22 October, section 5.14: its file by its place in the main folder, `Whiteboard/….png`, opened as an image; a moved or deleted one is a broken link, "Drawing no longer in the Whiteboard folder"). Each link is marked **Evidence**, **Counterevidence** or **Relevant** (Josh's word for "kept with it, no direction"), with an optional **note** and the day linked. Linking the same item again changes its mark (and note) rather than adding a second link. A link whose item has gone stays, in red, "Token no longer exists" and the like.
- **No shortcut makes a hypothesis:** they are made in the Cogitate window (**Make**, the green +) or with **Make a new hypothesis…** in the link picker. The word is always *make*, never *add*.
- **Link to hypothesis…** (Josh's Cogitate icon) in the right-click menus of All data, the matrix cells, the Utterances grid, the utterance cell, a text's lines (**Link line to hypothesis…**, the whole line), the token and utterance dialogues (the recording panel's menu, and a link under the fields) and the notebook's Questions, To Do and Comments tabs. It opens the **link picker**: what is being linked, a searchable list of hypotheses (newest first), **Link as** Evidence / Counterevidence / Relevant (the last choice remembered on this computer), an optional **note**, **Link**, and **Make a new hypothesis…**, which makes one with this link (one undo step) and opens it with the cursor in the summary.
- **The Cogitate window** (**View → Cogitate (hypotheses)**, or the 48 px button with Josh's icon between χμλ and the ?): a window of its own, its size remembered on this computer, the title in the theme colour and **Josh's icon at the top right** (88 px, as in the ɛλicit request dialogue). Two tabs, styled like the Matrix's:
  - **List:** No., Summary, Confidence, the counts of Evidence, Counterevidence and Relevant links, Last edited; the usual column filters, quick search and Clear all filters; **Make**, **Delete** (asks; links to it from other hypotheses stay, marked as gone) and **Edit**, which is the same as selecting a row and clicking the Hypothesis tab (or Enter, or a double-click).
  - **Hypothesis:** the number and the counts ("7 evidence · 2 counterevidence · 1 relevant"); **Summary**, **Formulation** and **Confidence** (Summary and Formulation one text size step above the usual, ×1.2, Josh, 22 October), saved when the field is left (one undo step), always into the hypothesis they were filled from (22 October: selecting another hypothesis in the List and opening it copied the shown one's summary and formulation over it); the **links** (Kind, Item, Note, Mark, Linked; Note before Mark, and the border between Item and Note can be dragged, Note taking the rest; Josh, 22 October), with a search, a filter Evidence / Counterevidence / Relevant or all, a filter by kind, and **Broken links** shown or hidden; double-click (or **Open**) goes to the item; **Unlink** (also Delete); right-click: Open, Mark as evidence / counterevidence / relevant, Edit note…, Unlink; a link's note can also be edited in its cell; **Link a hypothesis…** for hypothesis → hypothesis links; **Cited by**, the hypotheses that link to this one, with their mark (worked out, nothing stored twice; double-click opens one); **Back** and **Next** through the List's rows as filtered (Alt+← / Alt+→). Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y work in the window.
- **Going to a link:** a token is selected in All data, an utterance in Texts and utterances, a line opened in its text, a question or To Do in the notebook on its day and tab, a comment at its token or line, a hypothesis in the Hypothesis tab, a snapshot restored.
- **From the other side:** the token and utterance dialogues have, at the top right of their header row (under the fields until 25 October), a **48 px button with Josh's Cogitate icon**, Link to hypothesis… (Josh, 22 October: it replaced the words; a new token is saved first, so the link works), followed by **"In hypotheses: H3 (evidence), H5 (counterevidence)"**, each a link that opens it in Cogitate. Worked out from the notebook; nothing is stored on the token, and nothing shows in the grids or cells.
- **The notebook:** a hypothesis joins a day's session when it is made or edited that day (linking, unlinking and marking count), listed in the notebook's **Hypotheses** tab. **Lines in the day's notes** (Josh: automatically into the notebook entry's general text), under **=====COGITATE HISTORY=====** (section 3.7, History sections), editable like any note: one per change for made ("H3 made: Tone spreads rightward"), confidence ("H3 Tone spreads rightward: confidence initial → developing"), linked ("…: evidence linked, D001 goat · PL", "…: linked as relevant, …"), a mark changed ("…: now counterevidence, …"), unlinked and deleted; summary and formulation edits once per hypothesis per day ("H3 edited: …", or the made line itself on the day it was made), with the new summary. A day without a session gets one.
- **Undo:** every change is one undo step (`HypothesisCommand`: the hypotheses before and after, and the lines it wrote into the notes, which undo takes out again without touching anything else written there; a day made only for it goes again). - **Search and the archives:** the notebook's search finds a day by the hypotheses worked on it (number, summary, formulation, confidence, the links' notes and words), and the List's quick search searches the hypotheses. ### 3.15 Archives: sharing the data, keeping the researcher's own work (format 8)

*Designed with Josh and built 22 October (IDEAS.md idea 44, splitting idea 6's anonymous backup of 18 October).* The copy is `lemmatrix/anonymise.py` (no Qt); the dialogue is `ui/export_dialog.py`.

- **Why two:** an archive shares **the data** (section 1). Who may be named in it, and whose voice may be heard, follows the consent checkboxes (section 3.1); the researcher's own work stays out unless they choose otherwise. There are two archives because there are two kinds of reader:
  - **Anonymous archive** (File → Export → Anonymous archive…): nobody is named, for letting others see the setup: a colleague helping with the database's structure, or **an AI asked for help or analysis** (the dialogue says this is the one to use then).
  - **Ethical archive** (File → Export → Ethical archive…): the data as the consent forms allow it to be shared, for an archive deposit or a colleague: the raw data and the basic analysis that describes it (the tone classes, the tags, the lists).
- **What neither takes by default** (Josh, 22 October): the **notebook** (notes, questions, To Dos, Cogitate's hypotheses and their evidence), with a checkbox to include it, whose tooltip and the dialogue's note say why it is left out; and the **workspace**, always written blank. Later, **Analyze's results and Compose's papers** (section 6) stay out the same way. A **Full backup** (section 3.9) is the copy of everything, for the researcher alone.

| Language and variety | names become their codes; **Hide the language code too** and **Hide the variety code too**, two checkboxes (off), make the codes LXX and VXX, in the file names too | kept |
| People's codes | **renumbered**: SP1, SP2… and RS1, RS2…, in the lists' order, wherever a code is | kept |
| Participants | code only | **Identifiable?**: name and description kept; otherwise code only |
| Researchers | code only | the same rule, with their own Identifiable? |
| Emails | out | kept for Identifiable? researchers |
| Consent links | out | out (the forms travel only in the zip) |
| The owner | their new code (“Anonymous” if not a researcher) | kept, or their code if not Identifiable? |
| Recordings | **none**: a voice undoes the anonymising | only the speakers with **Audio sharing?** selected (a text's, only when all its participants did; the segment scratchpad's `Soundfiles/Segments`, a participant's only, never a researcher's or one with no speaker, section 3.9); the rest counted in the report |
| TextGrids | in, tier names renumbered, names replaced | in, names replaced |
| Places | top level by default (two levels or all can be chosen) | all by default (or the top two, or the top) |
| Notebook | off by default | off by default |
| Acknowledgements (section 3.1) | support, names replaced; **no thanks** | support; the **thanks only if Include the personal thank-yous** is selected (off), with the caution that everyone named must have given their informed consent |
| Workspace | blank | blank |
| Consent forms | left out, or a password-protected (AES) zip, which also holds **the key** (`key.csv`: kind, old code, new code) | left out, or the password-protected zip |

- **Renumbering** reaches every place a code is: the lists, the tokens' and utterances' speakers, texts' participants and researchers, the notebook's sessions when it is included, TextGrid tier names (a code that only a tier's `@speaker` knows is renumbered too), soundfile and TextGrid names (with a `{speaker}` pattern), the tier mapping, and free text. A code in both lists gets a participant code and a researcher code. **With the language code hidden**, the language's own ethnic group (the EACW's C + ISO code, `CBDI`, section 3.4) becomes `CLXX`, in its reference, file names and free text, a row of the key (25 October, the audit's P4). A new-looking code in free text that is nobody's old code (a stray `SP1` in an old comment) becomes `SP?`, since it would otherwise now mean someone else. The **key** is written only inside the password-protected zip, never in the clear; without the zip, nothing keeps it, and the report says the renumbering can't be undone from the archive.
- **Names replaced** (always in the Anonymous archive; a checkbox, on by default, in the Ethical one): every person's full name (any case), each part of 2 or more letters not written in lower case (scripts without capitals count) as written, and from 3 letters in capitals too (*JOSH*; 25 October, the audit's P6: before, *Li* and *Ng* and capitals were missed), and a researcher's email address, as whole words (a letter or digit may not touch them; an underscore may, so `KDB` is found in `LXX-KDB_D001.wav`), are replaced by their code (the new one in the Anonymous archive, together with the old codes and the language's and variety's names as written: *up the hill* isn't the variety Hill); in the Ethical archive only the names of people without Identifiable? selected, an identifiable person's full name staying whole even where a part of it is also a hidden person's. **Everywhere but the transcriptions** (Lemma, Phonetic, Phonemic, Morphemes, Pitch, the word class, and TextGrid tiers mapped to them): every value, open or closed fields, values of fields since deleted, closed fields' lists, tag and group descriptions, reference category names, the metadata's descriptions and owner, file names and labels, the tier mapping, the other TextGrid tiers (NFC first), clipped utterances' copies of their lines, and the notebook when included (snapshots' setups too). One pass, so a code put in is never replaced again. Settings this version doesn't know (kept as they are in the database) are left out, and the report says so. **Initials and lower-case name parts** (*J.*, *van*, *de*) aren't replaced on their own, since they would hit ordinary words: the report lists them (*Not replaced on their own: “J”, “van”*) to search the free text for.
- **Written** to a **new or empty folder** of its own (an earlier export can't leave anything behind; never the database's own folder or its Soundfiles), by default in Exports (`<stem> anonymous archive <date>`, `<stem> ethical archive <date>`, with (2), (3)… if taken; the stem with names and hidden codes already replaced, so the folder's name gives nothing away) as `<stem> (anonymous).xml` or `<stem> (ethical).xml`, with `.notebook` when selected and a blank `.workspace`, so it opens in Lemmatrix; the TextGrids and the Ethical archive's recordings in the fixed layout (`Soundfiles/…`). **Inside the consent forms' zip** the files are named `<code>/consent.pdf` (`consent-1`, `consent-2`… for a folder of them): a zip's file names can be read without the password, so an original file name, which may hold a name or an old code, never goes in.
- **The dialogue:** the two menu items open one dialogue with the archive chosen at the top (Anonymous archive, Ethical archive, in the theme colour) and that archive's defaults; switching applies the other's defaults (the folder too, unless it was changed). The report at the end lists what was renumbered or kept named, the recordings copied and left out, every replacement in free text, and that the notebook stayed out (it is the researcher's own work), with the reminder that free text can hold other identifying things.

### 3.16 Comparative wordlists: EACW, CAWL and Swadesh (formats 9, 11 and 12)

*Designed with Josh and built 22 October (IDEAS.md idea 40); the final EACW built in 23 October, after the list's review and final audit.* The lists and their mapping are `lemmatrix/wordlists/` (no Qt); the windows are `ui/wordlist_dialogs.py` and `ui/convert_dialog.py`.

- **Three lists, shipped with Lemmatrix, never in a database** (Josh: the list's own gloss "must be stored in Lemmatrix not in the XML database"):
  - the **Extended African Comparative Wordlist (EACW)**: Josh's own (his intellectual property), **a dataset of its own since 26 October**: its home is the repository **smojos/EACW** (https://github.com/smojos/EACW), archived on Zenodo, and it is cited as *Smolders, Joshua A. G. 2026. The Extended African Comparative Wordlist (EACW) [dataset]. Zenodo.* with the DOI for all editions, **10.5281/zenodo.23677135**, which always points to the newest (`wordlists.CITATIONS`, `wordlists.DOIS`: New database's hover, the Wordlist export's PDF, Help → About); each edition has its own DOI too (2026-10-24: 10.5281/zenodo.23677136, on its `Edition`), and the Wordlist export's PDF names the database's edition with it. The paper that describes it (*The Extended African Comparative Wordlist: A digital lexical organization system for field linguists*, in review as an SIL Electronic Working Paper) cites the dataset, not the other way round. **Lemmatrix's `eacw.tsv`, `cawl.tsv` and `swadesh.tsv` are copies of the dataset's newest edition**, shipped so that it works offline. **Its licence is CC BY 4.0** (Josh, 23 October, before the 0.2.0 release): `lemmatrix/wordlists/LICENSE-EACW.md` says so beside the list, for `eacw.tsv` and the EACW's links in the other two lists (their `eacw` columns), with NOTICE.md, THIRD-PARTY.md, the README, About and `.zenodo.json`; data, not code, so not under the AGPL. **The final list** (23 October): **2,556 items** in the reference categories of section 3.4 (C001f *mother*), **2,274 of them published** (Default yes) and 282 **hidden** (Josh's amendments of 24 October: B044f *female prostitute* the default rather than B044, C009 *child (offspring)* hidden, and C012–C024 hidden with their female and male the defaults) (recommended variants a researcher can add: female and male forms, generics, plurals, rarer contrasts). `eacw.tsv` gives each item its **gloss**, **word class**, **default**, **sub code** (blank: everyone; **A**: all of Africa; **N W E S C**: only those regions), **Clarification** (the gloss's plain meaning) and **taxon** with its family (*Capra hircus (Bovidae)*). The ethnic groups are a pattern (C + ISO code, section 3.4), not items. Nothing from Josh's data is in it;
  - the **SIL Comparative African Wordlist (CAWL)**: Snider & Roberts (2006), 1,700 items. Only its numbers, section numbers and the mapping are shipped: it states no licence, so its English and French glosses wait for SIL's permission (Josh is asking); `cawl.tsv` has the columns ready;
  - the **Swadesh list**: the 207 items combining Swadesh's 100- and 200-word lists (1952, 1955).
  - **The mapping** links the three (a CAWL or Swadesh item → its EACW items; Swadesh → CAWL). The EACW's links are the final list's, every one graded and checked in its audit: an underspecified CAWL or Swadesh item links to **every form it covers** (Swadesh *we* → R1PL, R1DU, R1PLIN, R1PLEX; CAWL *monkey* → the generic and the species), never to parts of a whole, other referents or another word class beside the exact item. Several lemmas may share a number; a CAWL–Swadesh pair with no direct row goes through the EACW. **The human terms' family links** (B and C; Josh, 24 October): an un-gendered CAWL or Swadesh item on the generic also links to its female and male (CAWL 0813 *rich man* → B047, B047f, B047m; catch-all, or the generic's own grade when weaker), and a gendered pair also links to the generic (CAWL 0345 *mother* → C001f and C001, 0344 *father* → C001m and C001; Swadesh 42, 43 likewise; graded possible); one on a gendered item that names no sex goes to the whole family (CAWL 0406 *soldier* → B033, B033f, B033m); CAWL 0338 *young man* goes to B015m only. **Not in a discrete family**, where the generic and its gendered forms each have links of their own (B001 *person, woman, man*; B004 *child, girl, boy*; C009, C010, C012, C016, C020, C021, C023, C024). **Elsewhere, a hidden item with no number of its own takes its gendered family's**; a published item keeps only its own.

## 4. Converting existing databases

- **No import or export of other programs' formats in the program yet** (LIFT and SFM are planned for v1, section 6). Existing databases, e.g. from Dekereke or FLEx, are converted with one-off scripts written for each database's own columns; they aren't part of this repository.
- **Dekereke's files**, for reference: a flat UTF-16 XML file (`<phon_data><data_form>`, with one element per column) plus a separate `*-DkUserSettings.xml`.
- **Tools → Convert references from a table…** (Josh, 23 October; `lemmatrix/convert_refs.py`, `ui/convert_dialog.py`): **a conversion map** for a database's references, e.g. one made from an earlier numbering of a wordlist moving to the new one. A CSV or tab-separated table with a header: **old**, **new** (**-** deletes a lemma, only one with no data), and optionally **gloss** and **old gloss** (the gloss, and the gloss in file names, change only where it is still the old one). A preview lists every change and anything that stops it (a lemma with data marked for deleting, two lemmas that would share a reference, a forbidden character); references in the table that the database doesn't have are listed and skipped. **Everything happens at once, by lemma** (references may swap places), with **Put the lemmas in the order of the EACW** (selected when most references are its items; one's own go to their place, section 3.16). Recordings whose names were up to date and pictures in Images are renamed with their lemmas, through temporary names; one undo step. **The table is the researcher's own: nothing of any database's conversion is kept in Lemmatrix** (Josh: a conversion shouldn't be "trapped in the code"; the tool stays for the next one).
- **A conversion** makes the lemmas and their tokens from the columns (e.g. a form and its pitch as the citation token, a plural column as a PL token), keeps the recordings' names (Lemmatrix shows those that are out of date, and Tools → Update all soundfile names renames them when wanted), and reports what it couldn't convert (columns left out, glosses or codes with characters forbidden in file names).

### 5.7 Saving and safety

- **Errors:** an unexpected error shows a message box and is added to `lemmatrix-errors.log` beside the program. **A crash inside Qt itself** closes Lemmatrix with no box (Josh, 23 October, opening the sample database with pitch hidden; not reproduced): since 23 October `faulthandler` writes where it was into `lemmatrix-crash.log` beside it, started afresh on each run, so it holds only the last one (empty after a normal run).
- **Undo and redo** are always available.
- **Timed autosave**, every 5 minutes by default (on since 18 October; Settings → General changes it, 0 turns it off).
- **Backup copies:** on each save, the previous version goes into the Backups folder next to the database (the notebook's too). Kept (Josh, 18 October): the **10 most recent**, and spread out in time: the newest of each of the last seven days (so yesterday's and the day before's, and one that becomes a week old), the newest of each week before that up to five weeks (so one becomes a month old), and the newest at least a month old: about 22 at most.
- **Saves are flushed to the disk** before they replace the old file, so a power cut or a dead battery leaves the old file or the new one, never half of one. **Every file Lemmatrix writes over an older one is written the same way** (25 October, the audit; `model.write_bytes_safely`, `model.sync_file`): under a temporary name, put on the disk, then swapped in. Before, only the database, notebook and workspace were; now also **recordings** (tokens, utterances, segments, ɛλicit's FLACs made into WAVs), **ɛλicit's received recordings** (on the disk before the Drive folder is deleted), its request zips and **review file** (it was written straight over the old one; a broken one read as no review, every card waiting again), **Full backups**, and the **TextGrids** Lemmatrix writes (line edits, Push to source, renamed tier codes). What goes into a recording is settled before it is written, so this never trims a take; it adds a few milliseconds.
- **A second Lemmatrix on this computer** gets a warning when it starts: "Another instance of Lemmatrix is currently running on this computer. Please save and close it before starting another to avoid bugs."
- **Full backups** (File → Full backup, section 3.9): the whole main folder as one dated zip, with a weekly reminder: the data and the researcher's own work alike (the notebook, the workspace), for the researcher alone. Sharing is the archives' job (section 3.15). *Save as* was removed on 18 October, with its warning about moving soundfiles.
- **Speed at full size** (25 October; the audit, section 3; `tests/test_speed.py`): measured on an invented database the size of a fully collected EACW (2,316 lemmas, 58,499 tokens, a soundfile each): opening 2.7 s (10.4 s before), an edit or undo 0.2 s, All data's quick search 0.16 s a keystroke (1 s for the first after a change; 2–3.5 s before), the lemma list's 0.06 s, saving 1.5 s (2.5 s), the XML viewer 4.4 s (10.5 s), Bulk edit's Fill predicted on 5,800 empty translations 2.4 s (153 s), inserting 2,300 EACW items 0.5 s (4.5 s); about 800 MB of memory, no recording held in it. How: the tag structure's lookups are cached and kept up to date by the structure itself (any change to a tag, group or their lists makes the index out of date, `model._TAG_GENERATION`); the soundfile folders' paths are remembered per database; the soundfile folder is listed again only when it has changed or Lemmatrix has become the active window again; names already as Praat writes them skip the opening rename check; diacritic folding skips plain ASCII and remembers the rest, and each row's searchable text is kept until the table changes; the database is written by a faster writer giving ElementTree's bytes exactly; the XML viewer colours the lines in view, not all of them, and isn't rebuilt twice on opening; the predictor checks the database once per Fill predicted, not once per token; the EACW's order table is worked out once. The English word lists load in the background three seconds after start-up. **Then (25 October, evening):** a text's lines find their segments by time (a 1,000-line text over 8,000 words: 0.83 s → 0.002 s); Texts and utterances lays a text's lines out once per change (twice before, and once more for each line edit), lists Soundfiles/Utterances only when it changed; the predictor is built once in a database with no translations yet (it rebuilt for every row drawn); the phonetics scratchpad's Segments listing is remembered until the folder or the codes change; the Lemma/Tag matrix finds an Exact column's tokens by their tags and measures each token, text and tag set once per layout (every lemma, all 92 columns: 25 s first view and 12 s after an edit → 5 s; with 12 columns about 2 s). **Measured at full size and fine:** Texts and utterances with 40 texts of 300 lines (opening 0.14 s, a text 0.2 s, an edit 0.3 s); a Bulk edit Replace across 51,000 tokens (planned 1.8 s, applied 3.9 s, undone 1.5 s; its undo step about 170 MB). **An edit no longer starts the grids again** (the audit, section 8, step 1): an edit, or its undo or redo, that changes lemmas in place (a form, a gloss, a tag, a Bulk edit) tells the grids which lemmas (`Document.emit_changed`); the lemma list and All data redraw only those rows (plus lemmas whose From names one of them), so a selection, the scroll position and the search's work on the other rows stay, and an edited row is filtered again; rows added, removed or moved, or any other change, start the table again as before. The Lemma/Tag matrix keeps each lemma's measured cells and measures again only the lemmas edited, unless something else that sizes a cell has changed (fields, Line up, zoom, fonts, IPA size, display conventions, tags), when it measures them all. On an invented database of 2,400 lemmas and 73,216 tokens: an edit in Lemma/Tag with 12 columns 1.8–2.2 s → 0.4–0.6 s (undo 2.0 s → 0.5 s), with all 92 columns 1.2–1.4 s (the layout itself is still built again); All data about 0.1–0.3 s as before, without the reset.

### 5.9 Predictive translation

*Agreed and built 18 October.* The engine is `lemmatrix/predict.py`.

- **What it does:** a token whose English Translation is empty gets a suggested translation, in grey italics in the field. **→** accepts it (the field is empty, so → does nothing there otherwise); typing anything replaces it. Nothing is written until it's accepted, and accepting is an ordinary edit (undo works).
- **English translations only:** the token's Translation field with `lang="en"`. Glosses are never predicted: the lemma's English gloss is what the suggestion is built from. Other translation languages get no suggestions (there is no Arabic translation field; anyone adding one is on their own).
- **Templates learned from the data:** for each token with an English translation, Lemmatrix looks for the lemma's English gloss in it as whole words (bracketed notes and any second gloss after ; , or / left out; a leading "to " ignored when the translation doesn't have it; inflected forms found with `lemminflect`, an ambiguous past form read as a participle after have, be or get) and turns it into a template for that token's tags. *father* with SG, 2POSS translated "your father" gives "your {gloss}" for SG + 2POSS, so *mother* with the same tags is offered "your mother". The gloss is inflected as the template needs (goat → goats, man → men, eat → ate); where it can't be, there's no suggestion.
- **Choosing a template:** the token's exact tag set first, with group defaults left out of the comparison (as in Exact columns), so a new token's Simple or isolation doesn't stop it matching converted data. Templates from lemmas of the same word class come first (Kinship before Relational before NOUN), then those from every lemma; if several compete, the most common one wins, and a tie goes to the template from the earliest lemma in the database. The tooltip says what the suggestion is based on (e.g. "from father, uncle, brother"). *Proposed:* when no template has the exact tags, one whose tags differ only in groups that don't change the translation (e.g. Phonological, Frames) is used. Combining templates (PL + GEN from a PL one and a GEN one) is left out for now.
- **Nothing stored:** the templates are worked out from the database when it opens, kept in memory, and updated straight away as translations are typed, changed or deleted. Nothing goes into the XML, and there is no side file.
- **No marker on accepted suggestions** (Josh, 18 October): an accepted suggestion is an ordinary translation and becomes evidence for further suggestions. A wrong one then turns up again and again, which makes it easier to notice; Bulk edit's Replace fixes it everywhere at once.
- **No AI:** everything runs offline in Lemmatrix, and no data leaves the computer.
- **Where** (when the primary gloss language is English):
  - the token dialogue's Translation field;
  - **All data** (Josh, 18 October: option 2): every empty Translation cell shows its suggestion in light grey italics, with what it's based on in the tooltip. It is only shown: the cell stays empty for filters, search, sorting and export. Double-clicking the cell (or F2) shows the suggestion in the editor too, and → accepts it; the right-click menu has **Accept suggested translation**. → outside editing still moves to the next column, as before;
  - Bulk edit's **Predict** tab (section 5.8).

### 5.10 Spelling

*Agreed and built 18 October.* The checking is `lemmatrix/spelling.py`; the windows' part is `ui/spellui.py` and `ui/spelling_dialog.py`.

- **What's checked:** the English gloss and the English translation only (utterances' too, since 19 October). Other languages, transcriptions, comments and the matrices aren't.
- **Not checked** (glossing conventions, not spelling): anything in brackets, words with digits, words in capitals (1SG, PL, NOUN) or with a capital inside them (xH), single letters, and words with letters outside English (IPA, Arabic). A possessive counts as its word (goat's, goats').
- **Two word lists, either is enough:** `pyspellchecker` (general English, the little words included, but American spellings only; also gives the corrections) and `lemminflect` (nouns, verbs and adjectives with all their forms, British spellings included: colour, favour, recognise). Corrections are words one change away (a letter added, dropped, changed or two swapped), most common first; Check spelling's chosen word also gets those two changes away.
- **The database's own word list**: since 21 October in its workspace (section 3.13), which travels with it (before: a `<spelling>` element in the database's settings), one `<word>` each, alphabetical; not written when empty. Adding a word doesn't mark the database unsaved. Words are matched without regard to capitals.
- **Underlines:** a red wavy line under each unknown word, in the token dialogue's Translation, the lemma dialogue's English gloss, the lemma list's English gloss and All data's Gloss and Translation (and their in-place editors). Not in the matrices (Josh, 18 October). The fields themselves are unchanged: a transparent layer on top draws the lines. **Settings → General → Warnings → Underline English words that may be misspelled** turns them off (per computer).
- **Right-click** on an underlined word (in a field or a cell): the corrections, which replace the word, and **Add “…” to the word list**.
- **Tools → Check spelling… (F7):** a window of its own beside the main one. Every unknown word, most frequent first, with how often, where (Gloss, Translation) and the first suggestion; under it the chosen word's occurrences (reference, lemma, field with the token's tags, text; double-click goes to the lemma or token). For each word: **Replace with** (a suggestion or typed) **everywhere**, one undo step, keeping a capital where the word had one; **Ignore** (kept in the workspace since 21 October; until then, until Lemmatrix closed); **Add to word list**. The list follows undo and other changes.
- Glosses are where predicted translations come from (section 5.9), so a misspelled gloss would spread into its suggestions; the check catches it at the source.

- **Later:** several tokens at once, from **Bulk edit only** (Josh, 19 October: All data stays one row at a time, or there'd be no point to Bulk edit), and turning a token into an utterance or the reverse.

- **Picking a tool:** a click on a marker or the duster in the tray (the things stay where they lie; Josh: lifting them would get unwieldy). Over the board, the cursor is a **circle the size and colour** of what you're drawing with (dashed for the eraser), with a dot at its centre when it is large. Each thing's tooltip says what it does.
- **Size:** the **mouse wheel over the board** (about 12% a notch): markers 1–48 px (4 to begin with), the eraser 8–160 px (40). Each tool keeps its own size.
- **The eraser rubs out where it passes**, like a real one (not whole strokes). **Double-clicking the duster** offers to **wipe** the board, as the **Wipe** button does: *Wipe the whiteboard?* with **Wipe** and **Cancel**.
- **Undo:** **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Y** (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) undo and redo strokes, Wipe included, inside the whiteboard only (not the database's undo), while the window is open.
- **Blackboard** (a switch, its icon a little board half white and half black): the board turns black (#1d1f1e) and every stroke already there is redrawn in chalk: **black → white, blue → cyan, red → pink, green → yellow**, and the markers in the tray change with them. A stroke remembers which marker drew it, never a colour.
- **A pen draws thinner when pressed lightly** (Josh asked whether pressure is cheap: it is, Qt gives the pen's pressure): from a quarter of the size up to the whole. The mouse and touch draw at full size.
- **Kept until wiped**, like a real board: the strokes (rounded to a tenth of a pixel; a pen's pressure per point), Blackboard, the sizes, the tool and the drawing last saved are in the database's **workspace** (`whiteboard`, section 3.13), so closing the window or Lemmatrix keeps the drawing, and another computer opening the database sees it.
- **The tray's buttons** (icon only, the names in the tooltips, on a lighter plate at the left of the tray): **Wipe** (the red ⊗), **Blackboard**, **Save** (the floppy in the theme colour; its arrow gives **Save as PNG** and **Save as JPEG**), **Copy** and **Link to hypothesis…** (the Cogitate icon).
- **Save** (also **Ctrl+S**) writes a PNG straight away, with no dialogue, into the main folder's **`Whiteboard/`** folder (made with the first drawing), named with the date and time: `Whiteboard 2026-10-22 12-30.png`, then `(2)`, `(3)`… within the same minute. **Save as JPEG** does the same as a `.jpg`. Saved **as it looks** (a Blackboard drawing black). **A notice at the top of the board** says where it went (*✓ Saved as … in the Whiteboard folder*, white on the theme colour, held 2.5 s then fading; Josh, 25 October: the tooltip it replaced didn't show); Copy shows *✓ Copied to the clipboard* the same way. A database never saved says to save it first. **File → Open folder → Whiteboard** opens the folder (greyed until it exists).
- **Copy** (also **Ctrl+C**) puts the drawing on the clipboard, as it looks, at the saving size: it pastes into Word, an email and so on (into the notebook's notes once images there exist, IDEAS.md idea 34).
- **Link to hypothesis…** saves the drawing first if it has new strokes since it was last saved (or that file has gone), then opens the usual link picker (section 3.14) with a **drawing** link to the saved file. Clicking the link in Cogitate opens the image. The whiteboard is not a viewer or editor (Josh: "You make it. You save it. You erase it."): a saved drawing never goes back onto the board.
- **Whose they are:** drawings are the researcher's own work, like the notebook (section 1): the `Whiteboard/` folder is in every **Full backup** and in **no archive** (section 3.15); a hypothesis's drawing link goes with the notebook when it is selected in, and points at a file the archive doesn't have.
- **Help → Keyboard shortcuts** lists the whiteboard's keys.
